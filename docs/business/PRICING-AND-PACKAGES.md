# Proposed offers, prices and billing rules

**September 12, 2026 · Founder-review proposal · USD · No public checkout or active Stripe prices.** The approved quote always defines the actual price, scope and schedule. The [machine-readable catalog](../../billing/catalog.json) is the single source for these proposed standard amounts. [Evidence and alternatives](PRICING-EVIDENCE.md) explain why these are testable starting decisions, not proven market-clearing prices.

## Two delivery lanes, one clear point of contact

**Brandon** leads websites, rapid development through his Aether AI workflow, backend systems, integrations and billing. **Edwin** leads media management, production, brand presentation and content scaling. Either can lead a mixed project, but both approve commitments in their lane. Parallel tools speed implementation; they do not eliminate discovery, client decisions, testing or maintenance.

Recommend four understandable entry offers, then introduce extensions only when a client's goal calls for them. Custom software is a discovery-led engagement, not an instant “build my app” purchase. The six public service categories remain useful for navigation; the billing system groups their work into finite commercial units.

## Four core offers

| Offer | Proposed full price | Due to start | Later collection | Clear boundary |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Website launch | $3,500 | $1,750 opening payment | $1,750 after acceptance, before final handoff | Up to 5 agreed routes/3 layout templates, one language, one contact workflow, search metadata, two design rounds, responsive QA and handoff |
| Development sprint | $4,500 | $2,250 opening payment | $2,250 after acceptance, before final handoff | One bounded feature/workflow in an understood codebase; accepted technical discovery and approved backlog required before quote |
| Content session | $1,200 | $600 opening payment | $600 after acceptance, before final exports | One 2-hour local shoot at one location; 12 edited photos and 2 vertical edits up to 30 seconds; two consolidated edit rounds |
| Social content cycle | $1,500 per month | $1,500 for the first agreed month | $1,500 per explicitly authorized renewal period | 8 static/carousel assets plus 4 short edits from supplied footage; captions, calendar, two platforms, two batch rounds and report |

The social cycle contains **12 original assets**, with adaptations cross-posted to at most two platforms: up to 24 placements, not 24 unique productions. A carousel is at most five panels and a short edit at most 30 seconds from client-supplied, usable footage. The shoot package's geography and shot list must be agreed before sale. Production work and monthly posting are separate; clients can select both, but paying one does not buy the other.

Fixed packages promise the agreed outputs, not an unannounced reduction in deliverables when our internal hours run over. Scope changes remain separately priced. Every creative offer specifies two consolidated review rounds; defects or failures to follow the approved brief are corrected without consuming a client's discretionary revision allowance.

Rush premiums, partner splits and company overhead are governed by the operating/partner economics documents; do not silently append a blanket “agency fee.”

## Why these prices, and where they are vulnerable

The [generated economics table](../../billing/ECONOMICS.md) uses the following assumed total effort:

| Offer | Base hours | Loaded internal cost/hour | Example effort allocation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Website launch | 28 | $65 | Scope 2; design 5; build 10; content integration 3; QA 5; handoff/coordination 3 |
| Development sprint | 32 | $70 | Scope refinement 3; implementation 18; tests 6; release/docs/coordination 5 |
| Content session | 10 | $45 | Plan 1; setup/local travel 2; shoot 2; photo edits 2; video edits 2; review/export 1 |
| Social content cycle | 16 | $45 | Plan 2; static/carousel work 4; short edits 6; captions/schedule 2; report/review 2 |
| Discovery | 4 | $65 | Workshop 1; bounded review 2; written scope and estimate 1 |

The catalog records corresponding estimates for each extension. These allocations include project coordination, not just production time. They need actual-delivery validation. Founder labor is valued even when no wage is withdrawn; counting it as free would exaggerate margin. Loaded-hour assumptions cover delivery compensation/burden allocation; general sales time, fixed company subscriptions, insurance and taxes still need funding from contribution.

For each engagement:

```text
Budgeted labor = base hours × loaded hourly cost × 1.15
Contribution = service price − budgeted labor − direct project costs − collection fees
Contribution margin = contribution ÷ service price
Price floor = (budgeted labor + direct costs + fixed collection fees)
              ÷ (1 − percentage collection fees − target contribution margin)
```

Round the floor up to the next $25. The default target is 35% contribution; care has a 30% target because it is a narrow repeatable service. The 15% labor allowance already covers expected labor variation; it is not net profit. A separately modeled partner risk reserve is deducted once from contribution/partner pricing, not repeatedly added to the same labor contingency. Partner jobs need their own actual contractor quote and retained-margin calculation; don't assume these founder-delivered estimates automatically work with a 70% subcontractor payout.

A $3,500 website budgets $2,093 labor, $50 direct costs and $116.10 collection fees, leaving **$1,240.90 contribution (35.45%)** before fixed overhead/tax. Its modeled floor is $3,475: there is only $25 of rounded price headroom, so habitual 10% discounts break the target. At 50% more base labor, contribution falls to **17.25%**. The landing-page model is similarly tight. Scope discipline and an honest estimate matter more than adding a premium adjective.

The content session has more modeled headroom, but travel, licensing and reshoots can consume it. Reserve the quote's planned direct costs, bill client-approved pass-through costs explicitly, and get a new estimate for changes. Provider/hosting/domain/API/advertising spend is excluded unless expressly included. Taxes are separate from service revenue, with actual treatment confirmed for the contracting entity and clients.

## Competitive choice and ownership cost

Published examples are not an interchangeable basket. [The full comparison](PRICING-EVIDENCE.md) includes lower-priced websites and social management. Black Star's launch is twice Tom the Designer's published five-page starting price. Oak Harbor's $175/month has a $2,100 first-year minimum. Black Star's one-time launch is **$3,500 plus client hosting/domain**; optional twelve months of $250 care makes it **$6,500 plus those provider costs**. Care and a subscription website differ in ownership, included edits and staffing; compare the actual agreement. Never imply that our optional care is needed to retain an already-paid site.

For a buyer needing one clear campaign page, recommend the $1,500 landing page instead of upselling five routes. For a business lacking usable images/footage, recommend a separately scoped production session before a recurring social contract. For existing technical debt, recommend discovery before a fixed sprint. The simplest honest recommendation is stronger than a larger but poorly fitted package.

## Booking, collections and monthly boundaries

Before an opening payment, require the scope version, named approver, asset dependencies and capacity confirmation. All two-part schedules above are 50% + 50% of the same total. Large custom projects can use a separately approved 40% / 30% / 30% schedule with exact cent amounts; there is no live generic large-project price.

Monthly prepayment buys one defined service period. It is neither a security deposit nor an annual commitment. Record start/end dates, renewal consent, cancellation method/deadline and treatment of unfinished work. Proposed default: client can stop renewal before the next period; no annual lock-in, no hidden setup fee, and no carrying unused care/improvement hours forward. Explain this when quoting; do not apply an undisclosed cancellation term. Publish no automated renewal until the client has expressly authorized it.

An accepted fixed price remains fixed unless the client approves a change. Do not change a past quote when the catalog changes, bill defects as additions, treat silence as acceptance, or claim money is received because someone opened checkout. Contract-specific refund/cancellation treatment and final handoff conditions are recorded before charging.

## Ratchet: improve the model with completed work

Start with one substantial technical build and one substantial creative production at a time; fit care and monthly cycles around demonstrated capacity. After each of the first five completed engagements, compare estimated and actual hours, total collections, fees, revisions, direct costs and client feedback. Preserve the result privately, with only anonymized aggregate lessons in Git.

If two consecutive jobs fall below their contribution target, stop selling that unchanged offer until scope/cost/price is corrected. If the client cannot clearly explain the output and next payment after reading the card, simplify it. Expand social volume, add specialists or introduce more price tiers only after proof that the core process delivers consistently. Fast development is a way to produce reliable value—not a reason to offer unlimited work.
