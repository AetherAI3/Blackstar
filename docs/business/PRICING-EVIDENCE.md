## Marketplace context: labor is not an agency package

[Upwork's web-developer cost page](https://www.upwork.com/hire/web-developers/cost/) displays a $15–$50/hour marketplace headline and separately describes expert work at $50–$200/hour. [Its social-media-manager page](https://www.upwork.com/hire/social-media-managers/cost/) displays $14–$35/hour. These heterogeneous marketplace figures do not establish a US agency median, imply equivalently qualified bidders, or include Black Star's account management, risk and delivery commitments.

The catalog's **$65 technical, $70 backend and $45 media loaded-hour costs are planning assumptions**, not quotations from those ranges. The proposed change-estimate rates—$125/hour web, $140/hour backend, $90/hour creative—are Black Star decisions derived from costs and contribution targets. Fixed package prices are based on scope and estimated total effort, not a claim that every working hour is billed at those rates. No sourced, scope-matched custom-development sprint price was established; that offer is cost-modeled and requires discovery.

## Evidence that would change the recommendation

Record privately for the first five **qualified** quotes per lane: selected package/scope, alternative the buyer actually considered, price, decision, stated reason, delivery hours, revision burden, net collected fees, and contribution. Do not fabricate a conversion baseline before leads exist. If two consecutive deliveries miss the contribution floor, reduce future scope or reprice before selling another of that offer. If credible buyers consistently choose a cheaper equivalent, narrow the entry offer or improve demonstrable value; do not assume they misunderstood quality.

Review fees before each catalog revision; revisit competitor pages within 30 days of making a public comparison. Keep catalog versions and accepted quote snapshots immutable. Public positioning should describe Black Star's own offer rather than name competitors or imply unverified superiority.
